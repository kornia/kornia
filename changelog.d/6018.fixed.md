`equalize_clahe` and `RandomClahe` now count, clip and accumulate the tile histograms of float16 and bfloat16 images in float32 on the default (fast) path, and cast only the finished lookup tables back. Before, with clipping enabled, a float16 image whose tiles hold about 65520 or more pixels, such as 2048 x 2048 with the default 8 x 8 grid, came back entirely NaN; a float16 `clip_limit` whose per-bin cap exceeded 65504 raised an overflow error; and smaller half-precision tiles got lookup tables one or more gray levels off in float16 and several in bfloat16. The lookup tables now equal those of a float32 image with the same pixel values. float32 and float64 results, and the `slow_and_differentiable` path, are unchanged.
