`equalize`, `equalize3d`, `RandomEqualize` and `RandomEqualize3D` now count each pixel of a float16 or bfloat16 image in the histogram bin of the lookup-table entry it reads. The bin was computed in the input dtype, where an 8-bit level v rounds up to bin v + 1 for v >= 240 in float16 and v >= 128 in bfloat16, so neighbouring levels merged and the whole table moved: `[10, 100, 254, 255] / 255` equalized to `[0, 150, 255, 255]` instead of the `[0, 100, 200, 255]` that float32 and `PIL.ImageOps.equalize` give, and a random 64 x 64 8-bit image was up to 16 gray levels off. Half-precision results on 8-bit images now match float32 up to output rounding; float32 and float64 results are unchanged.
