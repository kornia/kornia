`guided_blur(..., subsample=s)` and `GuidedBlur(..., subsample=s)` now accept a height or width that is not a multiple of `s`, or is shorter than `s`. They raised a broadcast `RuntimeError` (1-channel guidance) or an `einsum` error (multi-channel guidance) when the height or width was not a multiple of `s`, because the upsampled coefficient maps came out `floor(H / s) * s` pixels tall and wide; an axis shorter than `s` failed earlier, in a zero-sized `interpolate`. The maps are now resized to the input's own size, and an axis shorter than `s` is subsampled to one pixel instead of none. For a size that is not a multiple of `s`, row `i` of the subsampled image is pixel `floor(i * H / rows)`, and likewise for columns. Sizes that worked give bit-identical results. The box blur still runs on the subsampled grid, so its padding limit applies there: a call raises the padding `RuntimeError` when the window `(kernel_size - 1) // s + 1` is too wide for that grid to be padded in the chosen `border_type`, as `box_blur` does on it. The default `"reflect"` hits this first, since it cannot pad a one-pixel axis at all, and an axis shorter than `s` is one pixel; `"replicate"` and `"constant"` work at every size.
