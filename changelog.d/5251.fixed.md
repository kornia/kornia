`kornia.core.ops.eye_like` and `vec_like` now reject a non-integer `n` with a `TypeCheckError` that names `n` ("n must be an integer. Got: 2.0 (float)"), carrying the offending type as `actual_type` and `int` as `expected_type`. Before, a `float`, `True`, a NumPy float or bool scalar or a float tensor got past the `n > 0` check and failed inside torch with a builtin `TypeError` about the signature of `torch.eye` or `torch.zeros`; a string or `None` failed in that comparison with a `TypeError`; `torch.tensor(True)` and a multi-element tensor failed with a `RuntimeError`; and `False` and `torch.tensor(False)` raised "n must be positive. Got: False". With NumPy 1.x, whose bool scalars still implement `__index__`, `np.True_` was read as 1 and `np.False_` raised "n must be positive"; both are rejected too. `TypeCheckError` is a `BaseError`, and since #5233 also a `TypeError`, so both `except BaseError` and `except TypeError` around such a call catch it. An `int`, a NumPy integer and a 0-d integer tensor are accepted as before, and `n <= 0` raises the same error as before.
