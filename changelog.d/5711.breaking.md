`AugmentationSequential` processed a mask in its image's dtype, so integer labels above that dtype's exact range were silently rounded: `2049` became `2048` next to a float16 image. It now processes integer and boolean masks in at least float32 (float64 next to a float64 image), so their labels come back intact. A label that is not exactly representable even there (above `2**24` next to a float32 or half-precision image) now raises `RuntimeError` instead of being rounded; remap the labels to smaller integers or use a float64 image where supported. Next to a float16 or bfloat16 image, a warped integer or boolean mask (rotation, affine, perspective, ...) is now resampled on a float32 grid, so it can differ from before at individual pixels; the half-precision resampling on CPU also picked wrong source pixels, including labels the mask does not contain. Floating masks keep the image's working dtype.
