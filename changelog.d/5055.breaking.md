`Hyperplane.through` now divides the cross product by its Euclidean norm, so the returned normal has unit length and `signed_distance`, `abs_distance` and `projection` are right for tilted planes. It used to divide by the p = -1 "norm", so the normal of a plane through three generic points was not unit: for the plane through (1, 0, 0), (0, 2, 0) and (0, 0, 3) it was (-6, -3, -2), and distances came out 7 times too large. Axis-aligned planes, whose cross product has a zero component, used to take the SVD fallback, whose sign is arbitrary; they now take the cross-product branch, so their normal is `(p2 - p0) x (p1 - p0)` normalised and can be the negation of the previous one, which flips the sign of `signed_distance`: the plane through (0, 0, 1), (1, 0, 1) and (0, 1, 1) now has the normal (0, 0, -1) instead of (0, 0, 1). The collinearity threshold is now evaluated per batch row, relative to the dtype's machine epsilon, instead of once over the whole batch.
