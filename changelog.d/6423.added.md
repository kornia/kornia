`ALIKED` computes the per-keypoint convolutions of its descriptor head (SDDH) as matrix products with the same weights. At 2048 keypoints on a 480 x 640 image, this makes the head about 3x faster and the whole forward about 1.5x faster on an Apple M1 CPU, where `conv2d` sent these inputs to NNPACK. On an x86 CPU (i7-14700K), which uses oneDNN, the head and the forward are about 5 % faster, and on CUDA the head is about 10 % faster. On CPU the descriptors change at float32 rounding level only. On CUDA they move closer to float64, because the convolutions ran in TF32 by default (`torch.backends.cudnn.allow_tf32`) and the matrix products do not (`torch.backends.cuda.matmul.allow_tf32` is off by default).
