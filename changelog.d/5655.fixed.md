`equalize_clahe` and `RandomClahe` spread the residual of the clipped pixels over every `max(256 // residual, 1)`-th
histogram bin from bin 0, as OpenCV does, instead of writing it into the first `residual` bins. The old placement
lifted the whole lookup table, so small tiles came out tens of levels brighter than OpenCV's CLAHE (+38 levels on a
96 x 96 image with an 8 x 8 grid and clip 2.0); the output changes wherever clipping leaves a residual (#5654).
