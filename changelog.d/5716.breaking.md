`equalize_clahe` and `RandomClahe` now blend neighbouring tile lookup tables with weight steps of `1 / tile_size`, instead of `1 / (tile_size - 1)`, anchored at each tile's actual centre. This changes interpolated output values in both the fast and differentiable paths. Kornia intentionally anchors the blend half a pixel before OpenCV/scikit-image's historical phase to preserve flip symmetry on unpadded grids. For the same tiles and lookup tables, the phase difference is at most half an interpolation step per axis; exact `cv2.createCLAHE` parity is not the contract. On the images we measured with an 8 x 8 grid and even tiles (64 x 80 to 512 x 512, clip limits 2 and 40), the largest difference from `cv2.createCLAHE` fell to between one half and two thirds of what it was (21 instead of 39 gray levels on a smooth 64 x 80 image at clip limit 40, 3 instead of 5 on the 512 x 512 `camera` image), while the mean difference stayed close to what it was (7.0 instead of 7.6 and 0.54 instead of 0.57 levels), because the half-pixel offset from OpenCV's phase changes every blended pixel. Border regions still clamp to the nearest tile along the border axis. LUT calculation and quantization are unchanged.
