`tversky_loss` and `TverskyLoss` raise `TypeError` when `target` is not a tensor; it used to be an `AttributeError`, or a misleading device-mismatch `ValueError` for a NumPy array. They raise `ValueError` when `alpha`, `beta` or `eps` is a Python `int` or `float` that is negative, infinite or NaN (`TverskyLoss` at construction): a NaN weight returned a NaN loss, a negative one returned a meaningless loss (`alpha=-0.5` could give a loss below 0), and an infinite one returned 1. Tensor-valued `alpha`, `beta` and `eps`, including one weight per class, are not checked and work as before.
