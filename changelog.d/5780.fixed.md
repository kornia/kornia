`psnr`, `psnr_loss` and `PSNRLoss` compute float16 and bfloat16 images in float32 and return the result in the input dtype. In float16, `max_val**2 / mse` overflowed, so every PSNR above 48.16 dB (8-bit quantization of a float image alone gives about 59 dB) came back as `inf` and `psnr_loss` gradients were NaN.
