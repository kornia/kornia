`equalize_clahe` and `RandomClahe` now use tiles of ceil(H / grid_size[0]) by ceil(W / grid_size[1]) pixels, odd or even, and reflect-pad only as needed to complete the grid. Previously, odd tile sizes were rounded up to the next even size and the image was padded to fit, even when the grid divided it. For example, an 8 x 8 grid on a 72 x 88 image used 10 x 12 tiles over an 80 x 96 padded image, shifting tile boundaries and changing output values. These are OpenCV's tiles when the grid divides both image axes or neither. When it divides only one, OpenCV also pads that axis, by one pixel per tile, and Kornia does not, so on such images the difference from `cv2.createCLAHE` can grow. On the smooth images we measured with an 8 x 8 grid at clip limit 2, the mean / max difference from `cv2.createCLAHE` fell from 8.0 / 47 to 3.2 / 10 gray levels at 72 x 88 and from 18.8 / 82 to 1.0 / 3 at 100 x 150, and odd tiles on a grid that divides the image are now flip-symmetric.
