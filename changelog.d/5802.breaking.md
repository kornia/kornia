`resize_to_be_divisible` rounds a side of at most half of `divisible_factor` up to `divisible_factor` instead of down to 0: a `(1, 3, 10, 14)` image with factor 24 now gives `(1, 3, 24, 24)` where it gave an empty `(1, 3, 0, 24)` image. An empty side still stays empty. It reads height and width from the last two axes, so `(H, W)` inputs and inputs with more than one leading axis are resized like in `resize`; they used to raise `UnboundLocalError`. A `divisible_factor` that is not a positive integer raises `ValueError`: a negative factor used to give the size of its absolute value, 0 raised `ZeroDivisionError` and a float raised `TypeError`.
